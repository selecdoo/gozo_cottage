Authentic Gozitan Produce & Artisanal Delicacies | Gozo Cottage

Gozo Cottage (Cottage Products (Gozo) Ltd) is a food producer in Xewkija on the island of Gozo, Malta. It makes olive oil, liqueurs, marmalades, wine, cheese, salami and other traditional products from fresh produce grown and harvested on Gozo.

Gozo Cottage was born on Gozo, the isle of Calypso in the Maltese Archipelago. Its purpose is to use and promote local Gozitan produce and to bring old traditional recipes back to life, always made from products grown and harvested within the island region of Gozo by Gozitan farmers.

From the cellar & the citrus orchards

Traditional Gozo Wines & Liqueurs

Gozo Cottage offers a Merlot red wine and five traditional liqueurs made from Gozo tangerines, oranges, lemons, carob, wild herbs and almonds.

Wine from Gozo Cottage

Gozo Cottage’s project description includes a winery and cellars, so that consumers can drink locally produced Gozitan wine instead of imports. The photographs below show stainless-steel tanks, an oak barrel, a clay amphora branded Gozo Cottage and a bottle of Merlot.

Liqueurs from Gozo fruit, carob and wild herbs

All five liqueurs are made only from natural ingredients with no artificial colouring or flavouring, so the colour can differ slightly between batches. The citrus comes from century-old orchards in the north-east of Gozo.

  • Citrus

    Mandolin

    Tangerine liqueur from the old citrus orchards of north-eastern Gozo, harvested in winter. Fresh and fruity with a hint of bitterness: an after-meal drink.

    Water, pure hydrated alcohol, sugar, tangerines of Gozo.

  • Citrus

    Laring

    Orange liqueur that captures the freshness of Gozo oranges. Serve as an aperitif or after a meal, well chilled, straight or on the rocks.

    Water, pure hydrated alcohol, sugar, oranges of Gozo.

  • Citrus

    Limuncell

    Lemon liqueur from lemon orchards in Gozo’s north-eastern valleys, grown without pesticides and nourished by natural springs. Serve very well chilled after a meal.

    Water, pure hydrated alcohol, sugar, lemons of Gozo.

  • Herbs & carob

    Morr (bitter-sweet)

    “Morr” means “bitter” in Maltese. An infusion of wild herbs, bitter orange and renowned Gozo carob, best on the rocks as an aperitif or after meals.

  • Almond

Artisanal Jams & Olive Oils

Gozo Cottage presses Gigantija extra virgin olive oil from Gozo-grown olives and makes six marmalades and jams, local Gozo jams from tangerine, orange, lemon, prickly pear, fig and pear.

Gigantija Extra Virgin Olive Oil

Gigantija is Gozo’s own extra virgin olive oil. Its name is a tribute to the Ġgantija temples on Gozo, which Gozo Cottage describes as the oldest free-standing stone structure in the world. The olives grow exclusively in family-run groves on Gozo, are carefully selected, hand-picked and pressed by mechanical means only.

About 7–8 kg of olives are needed for one litre of extra virgin olive oil, and extraction takes roughly two hours. The sun, the fertile soil and a meticulous pressing process give the oil its freshness and fruitiness.

Marmalades & jams from Gozo fruit

Every jar is made only from natural ingredients with no artificial colouring or flavourings. The fruit is carefully selected, hand-picked and processed on the same day. Five of the six recipes contain 43 % fruit.

Citrus comes from centuries-old orchards in the north-eastern valleys of Gozo, where the tangerines ripen on the tree and are harvested in the winter months.

    Tangerine (43 %), sugar, glucose syrup, water, natural gelling agent, lemon zest.

    Oranges from centuries-old orchards in a north-eastern Gozo valley.

    Orange (43 %), sugar, glucose syrup, water, natural gelling agent, lemon zest.

  • Lemon marmalade

Dairy, cured meat & the Gozo pantry

Handcrafted Gozo Cheeses & Cured Meats

Gozo Cottage makes peppered cheese from Gozo sheep’s milk and salami in three flavours, alongside sun-dried tomatoes, capers, honey, carob syrup and sea salt from the island.

Peppered sheep’s milk cheese

Gozo Cottage’s peppered cheeselets follow a traditional local recipe and use exclusively sheep’s milk from Gozo. The milk is processed to the highest hygiene standards, with no artificial flavouring, preservatives or colourings.

Ingredients: sheep’s milk of Gozo, vinegar, rennet, pepper, sea salt.

Salami: Syrah wine, pistachio and piccante

The cured-meat range on Gozo Cottage labels comes in three flavours: Syrah D.O.K. wine, pistachio and piccante.

Sun-dried tomatoes, tomato paste & capers

Tomatoes are Gozo’s main summer crop. For the sun-dried tomatoes they are cut in half, sprinkled with sea salt and left to dry in the summer sun, the Mediterranean way of keeping tomatoes for winter. The jars also show a Sweet Tomato Paste of Gozo.

Wild capers grow across the Gozitan countryside and have been part of the island’s diet for centuries. Gozo Cottage’s capers are hand-picked when fully mature and prepared to a traditional recipe.

Sun-dried tomatoes: tomatoes of Gozo, sea salt. Capers: wild capers of Gozo, vinegar, sea salt, water.

Honey, carob syrup & sea salt

  • Raw honey

    From north-eastern Gozo, where wild thyme, clover, citrus and carob blossoms grow. Pure, raw, unheated and unfiltered; it crystallises naturally over time.

  • Carob syrup

    Carob trees were introduced to the Maltese islands thousands of years ago. The fruit matures in mid-summer; in local tradition the syrup is stirred into hot water with lemon juice.

    Carob of Gozo, water, sugar.

  • Natural sea salt

    Formed by the natural evaporation of sea water in the Mediterranean sun, in man-made pools dating back to the Roman era at Xwejni Bay, Gozo.

Farming tradition on the Island of Gozo

Grown & Harvested in Gozo

Gozo’s produce is known for its taste because of a Mediterranean climate, fertile soil in terraced fields, fresh groundwater and the nearby sea, and Gozo Cottage uses only produce grown and harvested on the island.

Grown & harvested in the island region of Gozo

The Maltese Archipelago consists of three islands: Malta, Gozo and Comino. Malta, with its capital Valletta, is the administrative and commercial heart. Gozo, the second-largest island, is topographically quite different: less industrial, quaintly attractive for its rural way of life, and renowned for fine agricultural produce.

That reputation rests on sun, fresh groundwater and the sea close by. Gozitan farmers work fertile soil in terraced fields and family-run groves, and Gozo Cottage turns their harvest into Mediterranean gastronomy in Xewkija, not far from Victoria Gozo, the island’s capital.

The olive harvest: picked by hand

Olives are picked in Gozo from the end of September to about the middle of November. Whole families often gather at the grove, spread a big net under the tree and hand-pick the olives into baskets. At Gozo Cottage the selected olives are pressed within 24 hours.

Processed on the same day, in Gozo

All Gozo Cottage products share the same secret: the best agri-produce of Gozo, harvested and processed on the same day, with no added artificial flavours, preservatives or dyes. The company works from modern facilities in Gozo, where traditional recipes meet current technology, under strict HACCP hygiene rules.

Gozo Cottage lists these areas in its facility: winery and cellars, meat processing and ageing, dairy production with raw-milk reception, jam processing, olive oil production, packaging and a testing, research and tasting room.

Quick answer

What Are Traditional Food Products from Gozo?

Traditional Gozo food products are foods made on the island from local ingredients and old recipes, such as extra virgin olive oil, peppered sheep’s milk cheeselets, sun-dried tomatoes, capers, carob syrup, raw honey, sea salt, citrus and fig marmalades and herbal or citrus liqueurs.

For anyone looking for Gozo authentic produce, these foods belong to the Mediterranean gastronomy of the Maltese Archipelago, and because they are bottled, jarred or cured they also work as traditional Maltese souvenirs.

Straight answers

Frequently Asked Questions

What products does Gozo Cottage produce?

Gozo Cottage, based in Xewkija on the island of Gozo, produces Gigantija extra virgin olive oil, six marmalades and jams, five liqueurs including Limuncell and Morr, peppered sheep’s milk cheese, sun-dried tomatoes, capers, honey, carob syrup and sea salt. Its photos also show a Merlot and salami in three flavours.

Are Gozo Cottage products authentic to the island of Gozo?

Gozo Cottage states that all its products are genuine produce of Gozo, made from fresh agricultural products grown and harvested by Gozitan farmers and processed on the same day, with no artificial flavours, preservatives or dyes. One recipe, the almond liqueur Morr, also uses imported sweet almonds alongside bitter almonds from Gozo.

Can I buy Gozo Cottage wines, cheeses, and jams online or at trade fairs?

Gozo Cottage says its products are sold in many selected delicatessens and leading establishments across Malta and Gozo. Its website lists no online shop and no current trade fairs. For orders, trade or export enquiries, contact Cottage Products (Gozo) Ltd on +356 2156 4467 or info@gozocottage.com.

What makes Gozitan agricultural produce unique?

Gozitan produce combines sun, fertile soil in terraced fields, fresh groundwater and the nearby sea, which gives it a renowned taste. Gozo’s rural, less industrial way of life keeps family-run groves and hand-picking alive, for example in the olive harvest from the end of September to the middle of November.
